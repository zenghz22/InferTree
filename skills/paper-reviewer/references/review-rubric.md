# Review rubric

Evaluate only dimensions relevant to the manuscript and explain any dimension that cannot be assessed.

## Core dimensions

1. Problem and claims: Are the research question, scope, assumptions, and claimed contributions precise?
2. Technical soundness: Do the method, derivations, algorithms, and conclusions follow from the evidence?
3. Experimental design: Are datasets, splits, metrics, baselines, tuning budgets, ablations, and uncertainty handled fairly?
4. Novelty and related work: Are the closest works identified and are differences supported by reliable sources?
5. Reproducibility: Are implementation details, data, compute, seeds, and evaluation procedures sufficient?
6. Clarity: Can a qualified reader understand and audit the work without guessing missing steps?
7. Limitations and ethics: Are risks, failure modes, privacy, misuse, and affected populations addressed where relevant?

## Severity

- Fatal flaw: invalidates the central conclusion and cannot reasonably be repaired in revision.
- Major concern: could materially change a central claim or requires substantial new evidence.
- Minor concern: localized issue that does not overturn the central result.
- Question: clarification whose severity depends on the authors' answer.

Never increase severity merely to make the review appear rigorous.
