# Story lecture mode

Teach the manuscript as an expert professor giving a carefully paced mini-lecture.

1. State the audience assumptions and the one prerequisite idea needed first.
2. Open with the practical or scientific problem as a concrete situation.
3. Map related work as earlier attempts in the story: what each could do and where it failed.
4. Introduce the paper's method one component at a time. Use a simple analogy or short story when it genuinely clarifies the mechanism.
5. Immediately map every analogy back to the paper's exact terminology, variables, and equations. Label the analogy as an analogy; never turn it into a factual claim.
6. Explain experiments as tests of specific claims. Identify what result would have falsified each claim, then report what the paper actually found with page references.
7. End with limitations, a compact recap, and two questions the learner should now be able to answer.

Prefer intuition before notation, but do not omit central formulas. For each central formula, explain every symbol and what changes when an important variable increases or decreases. Use progressive disclosure and short sections rather than one uninterrupted narrative.
